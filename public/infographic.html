01 · THE LEAK WE KILL

Today's paper travels for weeks. That journey is the leak.

Written, printed, driven across the country, parked in strongrooms — weeks before anyone sits down. Every red dot is a place a phone camera has leaked it.

Weeks of exposure · hundreds of hands
02 · THE FIX

Shrink that journey to zero seconds.

Until the exam's first second there is no paper anywhere: no draft, no PDF, no print run. You cannot photograph, buy, or steal a thing that has not been created yet.

03 · THE KEY CEREMONY

Nine officials. Any five make the key — at zero, never before.

Each holds one fragment, generated on their own device. Five fragments combine into the key at T‑0. Four give you pure noise — so no single person can ever know it early.

Real Shamir 5‑of‑9 secret sharing
04 · WORLD RANDOMNESS

Mixed with a number nobody can know in advance.

A global network of ~20 organisations produces a fresh random value every 30 seconds. The 2:00 PM value doesn't exist at 1:59 — anywhere on Earth. seed = key + this number.

Even hacking all nine can't beat this
05 · SIMULTANEOUS UNLOCK

One tiny key lights up every centre in the same second.

Centres hold the locked paper in advance. The key fits in one SMS. It arrives in seconds, and every screen shows the paper together — same questions, shuffled per centre.

06 · IF SOMEONE STILL LEAKS

Every copy quietly names whoever leaked it.

THE TECHNOLOGY, EXPLAINED

None of this is magic. Five old, proven ideas.

Every piece below is decades-old, audited cryptography already running in banks, blockchains, and browsers. We just arranged them for exams. Scroll on.

TECH 01 · SHAMIR SECRET SHARING

Split a secret so any 5 of 9 pieces rebuild it — 4 reveal nothing.

A curve of degree 4 is pinned down by exactly 5 points. Give each official one point. Five of them reconstruct the curve — and read the secret where it crosses zero. With only four, infinitely many curves fit, so the secret could be anything. That gap is the security.

Adi Shamir, 1979 · the "S" in RSA
TECH 02 · DRAND — PUBLIC RANDOMNESS

A dice roll no one controls, and no one can predict.

No single machine rolls the dice — ~20 organisations each contribute, and their pieces combine into one value every 30 seconds. The next value literally does not exist until they produce it, so it can't be known, bought, or predicted in advance. We publish which round we'll use, so anyone can check it later.

League of Entropy · runs live at drand.love
TECH 03 · COMMIT‑REVEAL

Lock a promise in public first. Prove you kept it after.

A hash is a one-way fingerprint: easy to compute forwards, impossible to reverse. We publish the fingerprint of the whole setup before the exam — it gives away none of the contents. Afterwards we reveal everything, and anyone re-computes the fingerprint. Same value means we couldn't have changed anything in between.

The trick behind sealed-bid auctions & coin flips
TECH 04 · HASH‑CHAINED LEDGER

A log where changing one line breaks every line after it.

Each entry in the audit log carries the fingerprint of the one before it — like Lego bricks locked together. Rewrite an old event and its fingerprint changes, which breaks the "prev" reference in the next event, and the next, visibly, all the way down. That's exactly how a blockchain stays tamper-evident.

The core idea inside every blockchain
TECH 05 · INVISIBLE WATERMARKING

The same question, carrying a hidden serial number.

Between the letters we weave zero-width characters — real Unicode symbols that take up no space and never show on screen. Arranged as ones and zeros, they spell out the centre's ID. Copy the text, screenshot it, retype it — the hidden serial rides along, and the tracer reads it straight back.

Same idea as "traitor tracing" in streaming & PDFs
PUTTING IT TOGETHER

Old, boring, proven parts. One new arrangement.

Shamir hides the key among nine people. drand makes it unknowable early. Commit-reveal proves fairness. The hash chain makes tampering visible. Watermarking catches the rare in-room leaker. Together: a paper that is born in public, verifiable by anyone, and impossible to steal before it exists.
