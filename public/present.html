ZEROLEAK — a live paper system for NEET

The exam paper that cannot leak.

Twenty-four lakh students sit NEET on the same afternoon. ZEROLEAK makes the one paper they all face impossible to steal — because until the exam starts, it does not exist.

The problem

Today's paper exists weeks before the exam.

The paper is written, printed, driven across the country in trucks, and parked in strongrooms — weeks before anyone sits the exam. Every leak in history happened somewhere in that long wait.

  • Hundreds of people touch the paper before exam day — printers, drivers, guards, centre staff.
  • One phone camera anywhere in that chain is enough.
  • In 2024, the paper leaked from a centre in Hazaribagh — students had it the night before.
Newspaper press room, early 1900s — Library of Congress, Bain Collection, public domain
The insight

Make the wait zero. The paper is born at 2:00 PM.

Not better guarded — nonexistent. Until the first second of the exam there is no paper anywhere: no draft, no PDF, no print run, no trunk. You cannot photograph, buy, or steal a thing that has not been created yet.

  • Before 2:00 PM, the number of people who know the paper is zero.
  • There is nothing to guard, so there is nothing to leak.
Empty lecture hall — Paul The Writer, CC0 (Wikimedia Commons)
How it works

A tiny key unlocks every centre at the same second.

Centres get a locked box in advance. The key that opens it is only created at 2:00 PM — by several people at once, so no single person can ever know it early.

1. The question bank is publicFifty thousand approved questions, known to everyone. Knowing the bank just means knowing the syllabus.
2. A locked box reaches every centreDays early, each centre downloads the bank in encrypted form. Without the key it is unreadable, so transport needs no secrecy.
3. The key is born at 2:00 PMNine officials in nine cities each hold a fragment. Any five combine theirs on live camera to make the key. Four fragments give you nothing.
4. Every screen unlocks togetherThe key is tiny — it fits in one SMS. It reaches every centre in seconds and every screen in India shows the paper in the same instant.

If someone still tries

Every copy points back to whoever leaked it.

Each centre gets the same questions in its own order, with invisible fingerprints woven into the text. Paste a leaked photo or message into the tracer and it names the centre, the room, and the seat — in seconds.

  • The fingerprints survive retyping, screenshots, and photos.
  • A leak during the exam is nearly worthless anyway — the paper worth stealing was the early one, and that one never existed.

Exam day

For students, almost nothing changes.

Only where the questions come from is different. Everything a student touches stays the same.

1:30 PM — seated as usualID checks, OMR answer sheets handed out. Screens read "Sealed — no paper exists yet."
2:00 PM — the paper is bornThe key ceremony finishes on live broadcast and every screen lights up at once.
2:00–5:20 PM — same exam as todayQuestions on screen, answers bubbled on the same OMR paper sheet with the same pencil.
6:00 PM — everything publishedThe full paper and a fairness proof go public, so anyone can check nothing was rigged.
